When VoidDrift got close to a release, I had to decide what it would cost. Free with ads, a dollar or two, or something more. In May I wrote the decision down: $4.99, one purchase, no ads, no microtransactions, no DLC.

Here's the reasoning, and the parts I'm honestly not sure about.

## What I was choosing between

Mobile idle games mostly live in three bands:

- **Free with microtransactions.** Most of the market. Needs constant tuning, events and a team.
- **$0.99 to $2.99.** Low barrier, lots of downloads, and a lot of players who bounce in five minutes.
- **$4.99 and up.** Fewer buyers, but people who chose the game on purpose.

## Why free wasn't an option

Free-to-play is a business, not a price. It needs live operations, economy tuning, ad integration and a steady stream of content. I'm one person building on weekends. A free game that I can't keep feeding is a free game that slowly rots. I'd rather sell something finished.

## Why not $0.99

The cheap tier felt friendlier, and that was the trap. My game is a niche: a quiet sci-fi mining loop at the edge of a black hole, with a mystery that's deliberately left unexplained. It isn't for everyone. At a dollar, people buy without reading. Some of them want something else, and the ones who want something else are the ones who refund and leave a one-star review.

The decision record calls this high-intent filtering. A higher price asks the player to read the description first. The people who buy are the people the game was made for.

## Why exactly $4.99

The game has a complete loop and a couple of hours of content, not an endless grind. $4.99 is in line with small premium games on itch.io, which is the only store I'm targeting, and itch.io's audience is used to paying for small, odd, finished things. It's also enough per sale to matter for a solo developer.

## What I'm still unsure about

- **Whether the filtering effect is real.** It's a reasonable theory. I don't have the sales numbers yet to know.
- **Whether two to three hours is enough** for that price in the eyes of a mobile player, as opposed to an itch.io browser.
- **Discoverability.** A paid game gets fewer people through the door, and a niche game with fewer people through the door can go unseen.

I'd rather find those things out with a game that's honest about what it is than with one designed around ad breaks.
